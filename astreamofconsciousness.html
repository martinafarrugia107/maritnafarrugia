Could it be the motherboard?

January 14th 2026
(This was intended for an open call submission that never made it.)

Three years ago, I remember turning on my laptop and the pixels on my screen began dancing around the four corners, uninvited, leading to a sudden black screen of death. I remember frantically trying to turn my computer on and off again, looking for any damaged ports or a crack in the screen. I felt upset, especially as a freelancer losing my only tool that provided me an income.

I started to panic, thinking of ways to explain to my clients that my laptop had died on me. Of course, I would not be compensated for lost time, so I had to think fast. I searched for a few places that could repair my laptop, hopefully on the same day.

On my way to the repair shop, a sense of hopelessness came over me. I felt a bit silly for feeling so upset over my laptop being broken. I thought about not having a laptop for a month, being jobless for a month, having no support system. I thought about support systems, I thought of my family, I thought of my mother.

I sat in the repair shop amongst other people waiting for repairs, everyone clutching onto their broken devices. It reminded me of a waiting room at the vet, everyone holding onto their pets and placing them on their laps. When it got to my turn, I explained everything I knew:

It could be the touch bar.
There could be pressure on the screen.
Could it be the motherboard?
It could be the motherboard.

He noted down all of the symptoms, took my laptop and told me to pick it up in a week’s time. I felt anxious at the thought of a week. But my device needed attention, and so I left.

I remember texting my family about this, as they had helped me purchase this laptop during my bachelor’s degree. I knew my mother had zero knowledge about technology, but having something like this happen to me for the first time led me to text my family about it. I somehow felt comforted, even though my mother’s advice was to buy another one and not bother repairing it. She is quite passive and usually supports any decision I take, even if she has no clue what I am talking about. Sometimes, I wish she would ask me, “Mar, what does the touch bar do?” or “What is a motherboard?” I thought about this a lot; could the experiences of motherhood relate to the functions of a motherboard? Could my mother somehow relate to this?

The motherboard is the core, a system of shared knowledge from one device to another. Similarly, my grandmother passed down knowledge to my mother, then onto me. I remember the three of us knitting in one room, learning one pattern and performing that newly learned function all together.

Additionally, the motherboard holds memory; it helps firmware, giving it pre-conscious memory. It upgrades paths and evolves the system over generations. Similarly, mothers carry embodied knowledge and encode how things work. I often think about collective family experiences, or trauma in our family and how those experiences are implemented into the systems of future generations to come. Do I blame anyone in this scenario? People often blame the motherboard because it is hard to diagnose its issues when a device is reaching its end. The motherboard is blamed because every single piece of information has to pass through it, and similarly, so does the mother.

I try to have a level of understanding, putting myself in someone else’s shoes, as they say. I do this by also trying to repair the relationship I have with my mother through care. A motherboard requires maintenance, regulation and prevention of failure before it happens. The motherboard shuts down systems to prevent damage and overheating. Somehow, I do this with my mother as well, whether it is me trying to calm her down or her trying to calm me during stressful conversations.

I am reminded of my laptop, currently stacked with all the broken ones in the repair shop. We take care for granted and ignore it until something is not working properly anymore. I also take communication with my mother for granted, especially since I live abroad. I feel bad when she texts me that she went to the hospital, feeling a bit helpless being apart and not being present to prevent any damage that has been caused.

Fast forward to a week later, the repair shop told me they had taken apart the entire screen and found no issue. The issue was the motherboard; it had completely failed and the device was at a point of no return. It would require nine hundred euros to replace the motherboard, with possible complications along the way. I wished I could explain to my mother what a difficult situation this was, that my laptop was going through some form of organ transplant, requiring expensive medical attention, or it might be dumped into a graveyard with the rest of the e-waste accumulating in the shop. I wonder if she would understand these similarities, she would probably find it all quite amusing.
